"Taichi, what are you thinking about?"

As usual, right before the match, Oikawa Tōru leaned in for a hushed exchange with Kaedehara Taichi, the way he always used to back in their Aoba Johsai days.

"I was just observing something," Taichi said. "Looks like we're the shortest team in Group A."

"Why are you looking at me?" Oikawa took a cautious half-step back. "I'm not the shortest setter out there!"

"You really have the nerve to say that?" Taichi gave him a side glance.

Oikawa wasn't wrong—technically,only Kageyama Tobio and Miya Atsumu were shorter than him among the starting setters in this tournament…

"Taichi, what's the plan today?!" Loser asked excitedly. Even though it was his first Club World Championship too, something about following Taichi's lead just felt right.

All of San Juan's players turned to look at Taichi. Ever since he'd been confirmed as the team's ace during the South American Championship, their offensive rhythm had grown more and more outrageous.

No one had been able to stop San Juan's attacks.

Bad form?With Taichi on the court, such a thing didn't exist.

That nearly arrogant level of confidence he exuded had gradually infected the team through every match.

And now, they had no reservations about treating him as the absolute core of the team.

They believed in him—without question—as the strongest player in the world.

No one else even came close.

"Didn't everyone say Group A is the Group of Death?" Taichi smiled faintly. "Well, they're not wrong. It's just that—"

"We'rethe ones bringing death to them."

-----

"Taichi. Long time no see."

Yaku Morisuke appeared in front of Kaedehara Taichi, flanked by his "underlings."

"This time, we're the ones with the height advantage."

Back in their high school days, Nekoma had never once beaten Aoba Johsai, not in friendlies, and not in nationals. They had always been the smaller team, cornered into defense, ultimately defeated.

Now, the tide had turned.

Just as Yaku had sought revenge against Sakusa and others during training matches, there was only one opponent left for him to face again.

"Yaku-senpai, I heard youcrushedthose guys who stayed behind in Japan," Taichi said with a sweet, dangerous smile. "Surely that's not where your confidence is coming from?"

"Huh, you've got good sources," Yaku replied. "I figured they'd be too embarrassed to talk about that.Wait…was it Kunimi who told you?"

"Well, then you two will soon have something in common—losing to me."

"Looks like you didn't grow taller in Russia, Yaku-senpai," Taichi said cheerfully, "but your ego sure did."

"I'll help yourememberwhat it feels like to lose to me."

—"Let's have a good match."

They firmly shook hands.
