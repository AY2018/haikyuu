"Left hand again?" Zaytsev frowned deeply. And the feeling was completely different from before.

Trentino knew Kaedehara Taichi was one of the rare few who could attack with both hands. But from all the matches analysed by the coaching staff, he still mainly used his right hand. His left-handed spikes usually only made up about 10% of his attacks,neverexceeding 20%.

Only on serves did Kaedehara Taichi tend to use his left hand more often.

This was the normal case—even for ambidextrous attackers, it was impossible to freely switch hands in a real match. Otherwise, not only would it fail to trick opponents, but the spiking angle and touch would also be affected. Furthermore, it demanded extremely high precision from the setter, since the contact point for left-handed and right-handed attacks differed.

Yet, in this first set, over half of Kaedehara Taichi's spikes had been with his left hand!

"Why? Did he coordinate in advance with Oikawa to use his left hand as the main attack this set? But…what's the point of that?"

Zaytsev hated this foggy, unpredictable feeling.
